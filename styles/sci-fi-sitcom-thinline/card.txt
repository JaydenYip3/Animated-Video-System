Name: Sci-Fi Sitcom Thin Line
Family: TV Cartoon Looks
Look: Flat TV vector cartoon in the adult sci-fi sitcom manner. One thin, even, near-black ink line (#141211) on everything: 2px on characters and props, 1.5px on the set, 1.2px on small details, measured on screen so it stays the same width in a close-up as in a wide shot, never tapered and only faintly irregular. Fills are flat with no cel shadows and no gradients; depth comes from separate darker planes (a side face, an underside, a shadow band on the wall) and the set carries sponge speckle inside those shadow planes only. Sets are pale domestic interiors (olive wall, tan cabinets with salmon knobs, pale tiles, grey appliances) with plenty of small dressing, and their panels are inked as four strokes that run slightly past each corner. People (person()) are flat colour with no shading and are framed large, usually waist-up with heads a third of the frame height: a big head whose outline is hand-placed, never a clean circle or ellipse (flat runs joined by soft corners, the two sides not quite matching, nudged per character so no two heads are the same), in one of four outlines (a child's ball, slightly taller than wide with full cheeks and a broad round chin; an adult's capsule, 1.5 times as tall as wide with straight sides and a wide rounded jaw; a softer oval narrowing to a small chin; a tall narrow head tapering to a small squared chin), eyes set high on adult heads above a long lower face, huge plain white eyes that touch each other and deliberately do not match (one about 10% larger and set higher, rims that are lumpy ovals and not clean circles, the smaller eye carrying the bigger pupil, which drifts slightly outward), tiny ink-scribble pupils with a lumpy spiky edge (never geometric dots), a small hook of a nose, a one-line mouth set slightly off the centre line and slanted, a head that sits a few degrees crooked on the neck with the chin pushed to one side, one cheek fuller and one ear lower than the other (all fixed per character by quirks(), strength set by ODD), C-shaped ears, simple flat hair shapes; a short thin neck, narrow shoulders sloping from the neck in one line, tube limbs that taper slightly with soft elbow and knee bends, hands with a palm wider than the wrist, four fingers and an angled thumb; plain clothes as a slightly looser layer (sleeves wider than the arm with a hem, trousers bunching once at the ankle, one or two short fold lines), shoes with a toe and a flat sole band; weight on one leg with hips and shoulders tilted against each other. Adults are the child rig with a 1.45x body under a head of about the same size. Optional per-person details are a flat-crowned short-brimmed hat fitted to the head, forehead wrinkle lines, a goatee with bare skin around the lips, round dark glasses, under-eye lines and freckles. The series lead (CHARACTERS.vic) is a bald man with a very tall lined forehead under a dark flat-crowned hat, round dark glasses, a brown goatee, heavy lids and stern brows, a dark zip jacket and khaki trousers; he is defined once in the source and reused unchanged in every video. The open mouth is a large soft deep-maroon (#6B1F22) shape, flatter on top and round below, with a pink tongue and separate cream (#EDE7C6) teeth (short rounded-tip triangles) hanging from the upper lip with uneven gaps, smaller ones rising from the lower lip, none at the corners, cheek lines at both sides and a small chin curve. Creature characters (the water molecules) use the same eyes on flat round bodies. Lettering is a rounded bold face (Fredoka 700) with a flat fill, a thin ink outline and a short hard ink drop; labels are flat cream cards with the thin line. Soft glow appears only around light sources (the lit oven, a screen, energy).
Motion: Characters and props animate on twos (12 drawings a second) and act pose to pose: a pose snaps to the next with at most one in-between, with a slight idle sway and breathing bob, two-drawing blinks in which the smaller eye shuts one drawing late, snappy overshoot pops, jittery shakes and bonks. Arms reach with a two-bone bend (elbow down when pressing or holding something in front). Camera trucks and the iris wipe run smooth on ones. Signs drop in, squash on contact and spring back; arrows draw on as flat squiggles; key numbers slam in over a flat jagged burst with a stepped screen shake.
How it explains: The topic is staged as a sitcom scene under one 2D camera with two original characters in a two-shot (full figures in the default vertical 9:16 frame, waist-up in a horizontal 16:9 frame): the hook title shrinks into a corner sign, the adult presses start, the camera trucks in on the microwave, its control panel lifts off to show the magnetron, and a lime squiggle arrow carries the microwaves to a mug of coffee. A coffee-coloured iris opens on the mug and grows over the frame into the molecule world, where water molecules swing to follow a flipping field arrow while 2,450,000,000 slams in; they jostle and bonk as the coffee warms and a thermometer climbs, then a hard cut on the microwave's beep lands back in the kitchen with the adult holding the steaming mug and the kid pointing at it under the takeaway. People carry the action (pressing, pointing, holding, reacting) and step out of frame for the diagram beats; labels are cream signs on thin leader lines ending in a dot on the thing they name. Characters are always original designs, never the references' own.
